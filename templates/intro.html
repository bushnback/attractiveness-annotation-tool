Welcome to the Attractiveness Ranker

This tool covers two face datasets. The first is a private set of 84 Caucasian female faces — the primary target for pairwise ranking and manual stack ranking. The second is the SCUT-FBP5500 dataset, a published facial attractiveness study comprising 2,750 faces (2,000 Asian Female, 750 Caucasian Female). The broader objective of both datasets is the same: to understand how humans perceive and rate attractiveness, and ultimately to train machine learning models that can replicate those judgments. Sixty raters have already scored every SCUT face on a 1–5 scale — you can add your own ratings and compare your perception directly against theirs in the Rater Analysis section.

The workflow has three stages. You do not need to complete all three — each stage is useful on its own — but together they give you the most accurate result.

Stage 1 — Pairwise Face Comparison
Build a ranking from side-by-side choices

You are shown two faces at a time and click the one you find more attractive. A ranking algorithm recovers the full order from your choices. Two algorithms are available:

Bradley-Terry (BT) — recommended for accuracy

Fits a statistical model to all your comparisons at once and produces a precise score for every face. BT consistently gives better-calibrated rankings, but it needs volume to work well. Aim for at least 10 comparisons per face — roughly 840 clicks across the 84-face set. Results improve steadily as you add more data.

TrueSkill (TS) — useful when data is sparse

A Bayesian approach that updates a confidence interval for each face after every comparison. It produces a usable ranking much earlier — even after a few hundred clicks — but the intervals are wide and rankings are less stable than BT at the same data volume. A good starting point if you want early results to seed Stage 2.

Stage 2 — Face Stack Ranking
Review and refine the order by hand

Import your BT or TrueSkill ranking as a starting point, then adjust it manually — drag individual faces up or down, or use the zone panel to quickly drop a face into roughly the right region. You can also build the ranking from scratch if you prefer a completely manual approach. The final order is yours to own.

Stage 3 — Face Rating
Assign 1–5 ratings to match the study format

Once you have a ranking you are happy with, use Bulk Apply Rating to convert it into 1–5 ratings in one step — draw the boundary lines between rating bands and apply them across the whole set at once. Alternatively, use Rate Faces to score faces individually. Your ratings can then be compared directly against the 60 SCUT raters in the Rater Analysis section.
